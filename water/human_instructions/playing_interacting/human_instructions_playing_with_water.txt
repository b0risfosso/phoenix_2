Human Instructions for Playing and Interacting with Water Safely

Purpose

These instructions describe simple, safe ways to interact with water using everyday materials. The activities focus on observation, movement, pouring, floating, splashing, absorption, and flow.

General Safety

- Use clean water.
- Work on a washable surface, sink, bathtub, tray, or outdoor area.
- Keep water away from electrical outlets, chargers, computers, phones, lamps, and appliances.
- Wipe spills quickly to prevent slipping.
- Do not drink water used for experiments unless it came from a clean drinking source and stayed clean.
- Use adult supervision for young children.
- Do not leave buckets, tubs, or bowls of water unattended around small children or pets.
- Do not use hot water unless supervised.
- Do not mix water with unknown chemicals, cleaning products, or electrical devices.

Materials

- Bowl, basin, sink, tub, or large tray
- Clean water
- Cups or measuring cups
- Spoon, ladle, or small scoop
- Towel or paper towels
- Floating objects, such as corks, plastic caps, leaves, small toy boats, or foam
- Sinking objects, such as clean stones, coins, or metal washers
- Sponge, cloth, or paper towel
- Optional: food coloring, ice cubes, straws, funnels, tubes, or ramps

Activity 1: Pouring and Flow

1. Fill one cup with water.
2. Place an empty cup beside it.
3. Slowly pour water from one cup into the other.
4. Try pouring from different heights.
5. Observe how the stream changes when you pour slowly or quickly.
6. Repeat using a funnel or spoon.

Observation Questions

- Does the water stream stay smooth or break apart?
- What happens when the cup is tilted more?
- Does pouring from higher up create more splash?

Activity 2: Floating and Sinking

1. Fill a bowl or basin with water.
2. Place one object gently on the water surface.
3. Watch whether it floats or sinks.
4. Try several objects.
5. Sort the objects into two groups: floating and sinking.
6. Try pushing a floating object underwater and then letting go.

Observation Questions

- Which objects float?
- Which objects sink?
- Does shape matter?
- Does trapped air help something float?

Activity 3: Making Gentle Waves

1. Fill a basin or tub with a shallow layer of water.
2. Tap the water surface gently with one finger.
3. Watch the ripples move outward.
4. Move your hand slowly back and forth to make waves.
5. Place a floating object on the surface and watch how it moves with the waves.

Observation Questions

- How far do ripples travel?
- What happens when waves hit the edge of the container?
- Does a larger hand motion make taller waves?

Activity 4: Splash Test

1. Place a towel under a basin or work outside.
2. Drop a small object into the water from a low height.
3. Watch the splash.
4. Repeat from slightly different heights.
5. Compare small objects and larger objects.

Safety Note

Keep splashes small. Do not throw objects hard. Do not splash near people’s faces, electronics, or slippery floors.

Observation Questions

- What makes a bigger splash?
- Does object size matter?
- Does drop height matter?

Activity 5: Absorption

1. Pour a small amount of water onto a plate or tray.
2. Touch the water with a sponge, cloth, or paper towel.
3. Watch the water move into the material.
4. Squeeze the sponge or cloth over a cup.
5. Observe how much water comes back out.

Observation Questions

- Which material absorbs the most water?
- Which material releases water most easily?
- Does folding the material change absorption?

Activity 6: Water Path and Mini River

1. Place a tray on a slight slope using a book or folded towel under one side.
2. Pour a small amount of water at the high end.
3. Watch the water flow downhill.
4. Add small barriers, such as stones, bottle caps, or clay.
5. Observe how the water changes path.

Safety Note

Use a tray with raised edges so water does not spill onto the floor.

Observation Questions

- Which path does the water choose?
- What happens when an obstacle blocks the flow?
- Does a steeper slope make the water move faster?

Activity 7: Ice and Melting

1. Place an ice cube in a bowl.
2. Touch it briefly and notice that it feels cold.
3. Watch it melt over time.
4. Add another ice cube to warm water and compare the melting speed.
5. Add another ice cube to cold water and compare again.

Safety Note

Do not hold ice against skin for too long.

Observation Questions

- Where does the ice melt fastest?
- What happens to the water temperature?
- Does moving water melt ice faster?

Activity 8: Color Mixing in Water

1. Fill a clear cup or bowl with water.
2. Add one drop of food coloring.
3. Watch how the color spreads.
4. Stir gently with a spoon.
5. Add a second color and observe the mixture.

Safety Note

Food coloring can stain clothes, counters, and hands. Use a tray and wipe spills quickly.

Observation Questions

- Does the color spread by itself?
- What changes when you stir?
- What new color forms when two colors mix?

Clean-Up

1. Pour used water into a sink, drain, or outdoor soil area.
2. Dry the work surface.
3. Wipe the floor if any water spilled.
4. Put wet towels, cloths, or sponges somewhere they can dry.
5. Wash hands after the activity.

Simple Summary

Water can be poured, splashed, absorbed, frozen, melted, colored, floated on, and guided through paths. Safe water play is about observing how water moves, reacts, spreads, and changes while keeping the area clean, dry, and away from electricity.
