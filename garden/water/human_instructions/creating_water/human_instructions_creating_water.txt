Human Instructions for Creating Water Safely

The safest way to “create” water is not by chemically combining hydrogen and oxygen, because that can be explosive. Use a physical method such as condensation, melting ice, or distillation.

Method 1: Create Water by Condensation

Materials

- Metal cup or glass cup
- Ice cubes
- Warm room or warm outdoor air
- Small plate or tray
- Paper towel

Steps

1. Place the cup on a small plate or tray.
2. Fill the cup with ice cubes.
3. Wait several minutes.
4. Water droplets will form on the outside of the cup.
5. Let the droplets run down onto the plate.
6. Collect the droplets with a clean spoon or observe them as formed water.

What Is Happening

Water vapor in the air touches the cold cup. The vapor loses heat and changes from gas into liquid droplets. This is condensation.

water vapor in air -> cooled surface -> liquid water droplets


Method 2: Create Liquid Water by Melting Ice

Materials

- Ice cubes
- Cup or bowl
- Room-temperature space

Steps

1. Place ice cubes in a clean cup or bowl.
2. Leave them at room temperature.
3. Wait as heat from the room transfers into the ice.
4. The ice melts into liquid water.

What Is Happening

Ice is solid water. Adding heat changes it into liquid water.

solid water + heat -> liquid water


Method 3: Create Cleaner Water by Distillation

Materials

- Pot
- Water
- Heat source
- Metal lid
- Clean collection cup
- Ice, optional

Steps

1. Put water in a pot.
2. Heat the pot until steam forms.
3. Place a cool metal lid above the steam at an angle.
4. Let steam condense on the underside of the lid.
5. Let the droplets run into a clean collection cup.
6. Turn off the heat when finished.
7. Let all equipment cool before touching it.

What Is Happening

Heating water turns it into steam. Cooling the steam turns it back into liquid water. Some dissolved solids stay behind in the original pot.

liquid water -> steam -> cooled surface -> condensed liquid water


Important Safety Notes

- Do not try to make water by igniting hydrogen and oxygen.
- Do not use pressurized gases.
- Do not use flames, chemicals, or sealed containers for water creation experiments.
- Do not drink collected condensation or distilled water unless the equipment and source are food-safe and clean.
- Use adult supervision for any method involving heat.
