Human Instructions for Building an Electrical Circuit Neuron Model

Important Safety Notes

This is a low-voltage educational circuit model, not a biological neuron experiment.

- Use only low-voltage battery power.
- Do not connect this project to wall power.
- Do not use damaged batteries, exposed high-current power supplies, or mains electricity.
- Use resistors with LEDs to prevent burning them out.
- If components heat up, disconnect the battery immediately.
- Keep water and liquids away from the circuit.
- Young builders should work with adult supervision.

Project Goal

Build a simple electrical circuit that behaves like a neuron.

The model demonstrates:

- Input signals
- Membrane-like charge accumulation
- Leak/discharge
- Threshold firing
- Output signal
- Reset/refractory behavior
- Optional excitatory and inhibitory inputs

Biological Analogy

In a real neuron:

- Dendrites receive input.
- The soma integrates input.
- Membrane voltage rises toward threshold.
- If threshold is reached, the neuron fires an action potential.
- The neuron resets and briefly becomes harder to fire again.

In this circuit model:

- Push buttons act as input synapses.
- A capacitor stores charge like membrane voltage.
- A resistor leaks charge over time.
- A transistor or comparator detects threshold.
- An LED lights up when the neuron fires.
- A reset/discharge path brings the circuit back down.

Recommended Model Type

Use a simple capacitor-threshold LED model.

This is the safest and easiest version:

input buttons -> capacitor charges -> transistor/comparator threshold -> LED fires -> capacitor discharges

Materials

Basic Version

- Breadboard
- 3 V to 9 V battery pack
- Battery connector
- 1 LED
- 1 resistor for LED, usually 220 ohm to 1 kilo-ohm
- 1 capacitor, for example 100 uF to 1000 uF
- 1 charging resistor, for example 10 kilo-ohm to 100 kilo-ohm
- 1 leak resistor, for example 100 kilo-ohm to 1 mega-ohm
- 1 push button
- Jumper wires
- Optional: multimeter

Threshold Version with Transistor

- NPN transistor such as 2N3904 or 2N2222
- Base resistor, for example 10 kilo-ohm
- LED resistor, 220 ohm to 1 kilo-ohm
- Capacitor, 100 uF to 1000 uF
- Leak resistor, 100 kilo-ohm to 1 mega-ohm
- Push button input
- Breadboard and wires

Threshold Version with Comparator

- LM393 or LM358 module/chip
- Potentiometer for threshold setting
- LED output
- Capacitor input node
- Breadboard and wires

A comparator version gives a cleaner threshold, but a transistor version is simpler.

Suggested Component Values for a Simple Build

Power source:

- 5 V battery pack or 3xAA battery pack

Input charging resistor:

- 22 kilo-ohm

Leak resistor:

- 220 kilo-ohm

Capacitor:

- 470 uF electrolytic capacitor

LED resistor:

- 330 ohm

Transistor:

- 2N3904 or 2N2222 NPN transistor

Base resistor:

- 10 kilo-ohm

Important Capacitor Note

Electrolytic capacitors have polarity.

- The positive leg goes toward the charging/input side.
- The negative leg goes toward ground.
- Do not reverse the capacitor.

Step 1: Prepare the Breadboard

1. Place the breadboard on a dry table.
2. Connect the battery positive wire to the breadboard positive rail.
3. Connect the battery negative wire to the breadboard ground rail.
4. Do not connect the battery until the circuit is checked.

Step 2: Build the Membrane Node

The membrane node is the point where charge accumulates.

1. Place the capacitor on the breadboard.
2. Connect the capacitor negative leg to ground.
3. Leave the capacitor positive leg connected to an open row. This row is the membrane node.
4. Label this row "membrane voltage" or "Vm."

Biology analogy:

The capacitor represents the neuron membrane storing electrical charge.

Step 3: Add the Leak Resistor

The leak resistor slowly drains the capacitor.

1. Connect one side of the leak resistor to the membrane node.
2. Connect the other side of the leak resistor to ground.

Biology analogy:

The leak resistor represents membrane leak channels pulling the neuron back toward resting state.

Step 4: Add the Excitatory Input Button

This button charges the capacitor.

1. Connect one side of the push button to the positive power rail.
2. Connect the other side of the push button to the charging resistor.
3. Connect the other end of the charging resistor to the membrane node.

When the button is pressed, charge flows into the capacitor.

Biology analogy:

The button is an excitatory synaptic input. Pressing it raises membrane voltage.

Step 5: Add the Output LED with a Transistor

The transistor turns on the LED when the membrane node reaches enough voltage.

Basic NPN transistor setup:

1. Place the NPN transistor on the breadboard.
2. Identify the emitter, base, and collector pins using the transistor datasheet.
3. Connect the emitter to ground.
4. Connect the membrane node to the transistor base through a 10 kilo-ohm resistor.
5. Connect the LED anode to the positive rail through a 330 ohm resistor.
6. Connect the LED cathode to the transistor collector.

When the membrane node charges high enough, the transistor turns on and the LED lights.

Biology analogy:

The transistor threshold acts like the axon hillock. The LED acts like the neuron firing.

Step 6: Connect the Battery and Test

1. Check all connections.
2. Make sure the capacitor polarity is correct.
3. Make sure the LED has a resistor.
4. Connect the battery.
5. Press the input button briefly.
6. Watch the LED.
7. Press the button repeatedly.
8. The LED should light more strongly or turn on after enough charging.

Expected behavior:

- A short press may not fire the neuron.
- Repeated presses charge the capacitor.
- When the threshold is reached, the LED turns on.
- When input stops, the leak resistor slowly discharges the capacitor.

Step 7: Observe Membrane Voltage

Use a multimeter if available.

1. Set the multimeter to DC voltage.
2. Place the black probe on ground.
3. Place the red probe on the membrane node.
4. Press the input button.
5. Watch the voltage rise.
6. Release the button.
7. Watch the voltage slowly fall.

This shows a physical version of membrane voltage.

Step 8: Add a Reset Button

A reset button manually discharges the capacitor.

1. Connect one side of a second push button to the membrane node.
2. Connect the other side to ground.
3. Pressing this button drains the capacitor.

Biology analogy:

This represents reset after firing.

Step 9: Add a Refractory Period Manually

A simple manual refractory rule:

1. After the LED fires, press the reset button.
2. Wait 2 seconds before allowing another input.
3. During that time, the neuron is "refractory."

Optional physical version:

Add a second LED labeled "refractory" and turn it on manually with a switch after firing.

Step 10: Add an Inhibitory Input

An inhibitory input should reduce the membrane voltage.

Simple version:

1. Add a second push button.
2. Connect one side to the membrane node through a resistor, for example 10 kilo-ohm to 47 kilo-ohm.
3. Connect the other side to ground.
4. Pressing this button drains charge from the capacitor.

Biology analogy:

The inhibitory button lowers membrane voltage, making the neuron less likely to fire.

Step 11: Add Multiple Excitatory Inputs

To represent several dendrites:

1. Add two or three extra push buttons.
2. Each button connects from positive power to the membrane node through its own resistor.
3. Use different resistor values to represent different synaptic strengths.

Example:

- Strong excitatory input: 10 kilo-ohm resistor
- Medium excitatory input: 22 kilo-ohm resistor
- Weak excitatory input: 47 kilo-ohm resistor

Lower resistance means stronger charging.

Step 12: Add Adjustable Threshold

A comparator gives a cleaner adjustable threshold.

Comparator idea:

1. Feed the membrane node into one comparator input.
2. Feed an adjustable reference voltage into the other input using a potentiometer.
3. Comparator output drives the LED.
4. Turning the potentiometer changes the firing threshold.

Biology analogy:

The potentiometer changes how excitable the neuron is.

Step 13: Build a Small Neuron Network

After one neuron works, build two or three copies.

Network idea:

Neuron A output LED/transistor -> input resistor -> Neuron B membrane node

This lets one neuron help charge the next neuron.

Simple 3-neuron network:

- Neuron A: excitatory input neuron
- Neuron B: processing neuron
- Neuron C: output neuron

Add one inhibitory neuron:

- Neuron I: drains Neuron C's membrane node when activated

Network behavior:

- A firing makes B more likely to fire.
- B firing makes C more likely to fire.
- I firing makes C less likely to fire.

Step 14: Label the Circuit

Add paper labels:

- Dendrite input
- Excitatory synapse
- Inhibitory synapse
- Membrane capacitor
- Leak resistor
- Threshold transistor
- Output LED
- Reset
- Refractory period

Step 15: Run Experiments

Experiment 1: Single weak input

1. Press the excitatory button once.
2. Observe whether the LED fires.
3. If it does not fire, the signal was below threshold.

Experiment 2: Repeated input

1. Press the excitatory button several times.
2. Watch the capacitor voltage build.
3. Observe the LED firing.

Experiment 3: Leak rate

1. Try different leak resistor values.
2. A smaller resistor drains faster.
3. A larger resistor stores charge longer.

Experiment 4: Synaptic strength

1. Try different charging resistor values.
2. Lower resistance creates stronger input.
3. Higher resistance creates weaker input.

Experiment 5: Inhibition

1. Charge the neuron near threshold.
2. Press the inhibitory button.
3. Observe that voltage drops and firing is delayed or blocked.

Experiment 6: Threshold

1. If using a comparator, change the threshold potentiometer.
2. Higher threshold makes firing harder.
3. Lower threshold makes firing easier.

Troubleshooting

LED never lights:

- Check LED polarity.
- Check battery connection.
- Check transistor pinout.
- Check whether the capacitor voltage is rising.
- Try a smaller base resistor or larger charging time.
- Check that the LED has a current-limiting resistor.

LED always stays on:

- The membrane node may be permanently connected to positive power.
- The leak resistor may be too large.
- The transistor may be wired incorrectly.
- The threshold may be too low.

Capacitor does not charge:

- Check push button wiring.
- Check charging resistor connection.
- Check capacitor polarity.
- Check battery voltage.

Components get hot:

- Disconnect power immediately.
- Check for short circuits.
- Make sure the LED has a resistor.
- Make sure power and ground are not directly connected.

What the Model Demonstrates

- The capacitor stores charge like a neuron's membrane.
- The leak resistor slowly returns the neuron toward rest.
- Excitatory inputs raise voltage.
- Inhibitory inputs lower voltage.
- The transistor or comparator acts like a threshold detector.
- The LED shows firing.
- Reset/discharge models the neuron returning after a spike.
- Multiple circuit neurons can form a simple network.

Final Summary

This electrical circuit neuron model uses safe low-voltage components to represent how a neuron receives input, accumulates voltage, reaches threshold, fires an output, and resets. By adding more input buttons and multiple neuron circuits, the model can demonstrate excitatory signaling, inhibitory signaling, threshold behavior, refractory periods, and small neuron-network computation.
